# Literature comparison for P047

The following results are established predecessors; a negative exact-phrase search does not certify priority.

1. J. Mycielski, *Independent sets in topological algebras*, **Fundamenta Mathematicae** 55 (1964), 139–147, [doi:10.4064/fm-55-2-139-147](https://doi.org/10.4064/fm-55-2-139-147). Its category-theoretic perfect-set theorem applies to countably many nowhere-dense algebraic relations, but does not prescribe Hausdorff dimension.
2. M. Doležal and W. Kubiś, *Perfect independent sets with respect to infinitely many relations*, **Archive for Mathematical Logic** 55 (2016), 847–856, [doi:10.1007/s00153-016-0498-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00153-016-0498-3). Stronger formulations of perfect independence.
3. T. Keleti, *A 1-dimensional subset of the reals that intersects each of its translates in at most a single point*, **Real Analysis Exchange** 24 (1998/99), 843–844, [doi:10.2307/44153003](https://doi.org/10.2307/44153003). Full-dimensional additive Sidon behavior over the real field.
4. A. Máthé, *Sets of large dimension not containing polynomial configurations*, **Advances in Mathematics** 316 (2017), 691–709, [doi:10.1016/j.aim.2017.01.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2017.01.002). Quantitative pattern avoidance for polynomial relations.
5. R. Fraser, *Large subsets of local fields not containing configurations*, [arXiv:1612.08231](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.08231), Theorem 1.1. The theorem applies directly to the two nonzero polynomials $a+b-c-d$ and $ab-cd$, of maximum degree two, and gives a simultaneous avoiding set of Hausdorff dimension $1/2$. Applied just to $a+b-c-d$, it gives an additive four-point-avoiding set of dimension one. Neither conclusion settles the simultaneous dimension-one question.
6. Y. Jing and A. Mudgal, *Finding large additive and multiplicative Sidon sets in sets of integers*, **Mathematische Annalen** 391 (2025), 685–715, [doi:10.1007/s00208-024-02932-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00208-024-02932-7). Relevant arithmetic intersection of Sidon conditions.
7. T. Mátrai, *Infinite dimensional perfect set theorems*, **Transactions of the American Mathematical Society** 365 (2013), 23–58, [doi:10.1090/S0002-9947-2012-05468-7](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-2012-05468-7). Develops Banach–Mazur fusion games for infinite-dimensional perfect-set results; the game-theoretic generalization by itself is established prior art.
8. J. C. Oxtoby, *The Banach–Mazur game and the Banach category theorem*, in *Contributions to the Theory of Games*, vol. III, **Annals of Mathematics Studies** 39 (1957), 159–163. [Historical game background](https://encyclopediaofmath.org/wiki/Alpha-favourable_space).

9. J. Pach and D. Zakharov, *Ruzsa's Problem on Bi-Sidon Sets*, **Combinatorica** 45 (2025), article 26, [doi:10.1007/s00493-025-00151-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00493-025-00151-5). Studies finite real sets with simultaneous additive and multiplicative Sidon conditions. The characteristic-two, four-pairwise-distinct, local-field dimension question requires separate analysis.
10. A. Máthé and W. O'Regan, *Discretised sum-product theorems by Shannon-type inequalities*, **Journal of the London Mathematical Society** 112 (2025), e70389, [doi:10.1112/jlms.70389](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms.70389). The distinction between Hausdorff-dimensional and discrete-scale sum-product phenomena is relevant to the finite-scale obstruction.

**Priority status.** Unverified. Fraser's theorem has been compared at the level of its stated conclusion. A further specialist comparison is required before declaring the simultaneous dimension-one question historically new or open. Neither a general game on a hyperspace nor the existence of perfect algebraically independent sets should be presented as new.
