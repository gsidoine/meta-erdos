# The half-dimensional bound for proper closed subfields

Let $K$ be a nonarchimedean local field with residue field of order $q$, normalized valuation $v_K(K^\times)=\mathbb Z$, and metric $d(x,y)=q^{-v_K(x-y)}$ for distinct points.

**Proposition.** Let $L\subset K$ be an infinite closed subfield. Write $v_K(L^\times)=e\mathbb Z$ with $e\ge1$, and let the residue field of $L$ have $r$ elements. Then

$$
\dim_H L=\frac{\log r}{e\log q}.
$$

If $L\ne K$, then $\dim_H L\le1/2$.

**Proof.** The induced valuation on an infinite closed subfield is nontrivial: otherwise all distinct elements of $L$ would have distinct residues in the finite residue field of $K$, making $L$ finite. Thus the value group is $e\mathbb Z$ for a positive integer $e$. The residue field of $L$ embeds into that of $K$, so $r$ is a prime-power cardinality of a subfield of $\mathbb F_q$.

The valuation ring $O_L=L\cap O_K$ is compact. Let $\varpi\in L$ have $v_K(\varpi)=e$, and choose $r$ representatives in $O_L$ of its residue field. By completeness, every $x\in O_L$ has a unique expansion

$$
x=\sum_{j\ge0}c_j\varpi^j
$$

with these representatives. The $r^n$ cylinders specified by the first $n$ digits are pairwise disjoint balls in $O_L$ of ambient radius $q^{-en}$. The uniform probability measure gives each cylinder mass $r^{-n}$, proving by the covering and mass-distribution estimates that $\dim_H O_L=\log r/(e\log q)$. The field $L$ is a countable union of scalar multiples of $O_L$, so it has the same dimension.

If $e=1$ and $r=q$, the representatives cover the full residue field of $K$ and $\varpi$ is also a uniformizer of $K$. Repeating the digit expansion for any element of $O_K$ and using closedness gives $O_K\subset L$, hence $L=K$.

For a proper $L$, either $e\ge2$, which gives $\log r/(e\log q)\le1/2$, or $r<q$. In the latter case the finite-field subfield relation yields $r\le\sqrt q$, and again the dimension is at most $1/2$. Finite subfields have dimension zero. $\square$

**Sharpness in the power-series case.** For every finite field $\mathbb F_q$, the closed proper subfield $\mathbb F_q((T^2))\subset\mathbb F_q((T))$ has dimension $1/2$ in the $T$-adic metric. In $\mathbb F_4((T))$, the subfield $\mathbb F_2((T))$ gives another equality case.

**Corollary.** If $E\subset K$ has Hausdorff dimension greater than $1/2$, the subfield generated by $E$ is dense in $K$.

**Proof.** Its closure is a closed subfield containing $E$. The proposition excludes the possibility that it is proper. $\square$

**Consequence for P047.** No affine translate of a subset of a proper **closed** subfield of $\mathbb F_4((T))$ can have Hausdorff dimension greater than $1/2$. Thus the affine subfield separation lemma cannot improve the exponent merely by changing the closed subfield. The statement does not exclude nonclosed proper subfields or more general constructions.

The valuation-counting proof is elementary, and historical originality is not asserted.
