# Hausdorff dimensions of closed subfields of a Laurent-series field

Let $K=\mathbb F_4((T))$, with $v_T(T)=1$ and distance $d(x,y)=4^{-v_T(x-y)}$ for distinct $x,y$.

**Proposition.** If $L\subset K$ is an infinite closed subfield, let $v_T(L^\times)=e\mathbb Z$ for a positive integer $e$, and let its residue field have order $2^f$, where $f\in\{1,2\}$. Then

$$
\dim_H L=\frac{f}{2e}.
$$

If $L\ne K$, then $\dim_H L\le 1/2$. Equality is attained by $\mathbb F_2((T))$ and $\mathbb F_4((T^2))$.

**Proof.** The induced nonarchimedean valuation on an infinite closed subfield is nontrivial. Indeed, if every nonzero element had valuation zero, distinct elements of $L$ would have distinct reductions modulo $T$, forcing $|L|\le4$. Its nonzero value group is therefore $e\mathbb Z$, with $e\ge1$.

The valuation ring $O_L=L\cap\mathbb F_4[[T]]$ is compact. Let $\varpi\in L$ have $v_T(\varpi)=e$, and choose $2^f$ representatives in $O_L$ of its residue field, a subfield of $\mathbb F_4$. By completeness, every $x\in O_L$ has a unique expansion

$$
x=\sum_{j\ge0}r_j\varpi^j
$$

with those representatives. The $2^{fn}$ cylinders specified by the first $n$ digits are pairwise disjoint balls in $O_L$ of ambient radius $4^{-en}$. The uniform probability measure assigns each cylinder mass $2^{-fn}$. Comparing covers and masses at these discrete scales gives $\dim_H O_L=f/(2e)$. Since $L$ is a countable union of bounded scalar multiples of $O_L$, it has the same dimension.

If $f=2$ and $e=1$, the residue representatives cover every residue in $\mathbb F_4$, and $\varpi$ is also a uniformizer of $K$. Applying the digit expansion to any element of $\mathbb F_4[[T]]$ shows that it lies in the closed subfield $L$. Thus $L=K$.

For a proper infinite $L$, either $f=1$ or $e\ge2$, giving $f/(2e)\le1/2$. Finite subfields have dimension zero. The two displayed examples realize $(f,e)=(1,1)$ and $(2,2)$, respectively. $\square$

**Corollary.** If $E\subset K$ satisfies $\dim_H E>1/2$, the subfield generated by $E$ is dense in $K$.

**Proof.** Its closure is a closed subfield containing $E$. The proposition excludes its being proper. $\square$

**Consequence for P047.** If $L\subsetneq K$ is a closed subfield, then every translate $\lambda+S$ with $S\subset L$ has Hausdorff dimension at most $1/2$. Thus the affine subfield separation lemma cannot yield a larger-dimensional set by changing to a different proper **closed** subfield. It does not rule out nonclosed proper subfields or constructions not contained in affine subfields.

The valuation-counting argument is elementary; no claim of historical novelty is made.
