# Human–AI collaboration and mathematical credit

This independent, contemporary Scottish Book project studies whether collaboration between humans and modern AI systems can help **formulate** mathematical problems of lasting significance. Problem generation, verification and historical originality are separate questions.

Contributions may involve human-only formulation, human–AI discussion, AI-assisted exploratory computation, formal proof assistants, or human proofs subsequently checked with tools. Human participants and useful AI assistance should be documented according to their actual role, rather than inferred from a polished final text.

For a proposed problem, the submission form records AI involvement. For established entries, the original research provenance may be incompletely documented; no unsupported assignment of a particular theorem or idea to a named individual or tool should be inferred from repository format. Historical bibliographic attribution remains mandatory.

Every public problem must be independently intelligible. Mathematical statements, proofs, computations and references should contain their actual evidence, not references to a private conversational transcript. Honest disclosure of significant AI assistance is compatible with concise, impersonal mathematical exposition.

AI-generated arguments may contain errors or duplicate known work. A computed example is not an infinite proof. Passing Lean verifies a formal proof relative to the specified logical system and accepted dependencies; it does not certify originality or publication.

Submitted manuscripts and journal-specific AI-use disclosures are historical records. Their factual claims must not be silently altered by later changes in the repository's identity.
