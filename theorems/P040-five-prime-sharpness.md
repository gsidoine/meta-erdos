# P040 — Exact fractional Hall threshold for five odd primes

**Status:** computer-assisted theorem with an exact finite integer certificate. The unbounded-prime variational limit, the graph resilience, and historical novelty remain unresolved.

Let $P=\{3,5,7,11,13\}$. Equip $2^P$ with the product measure $\mu$ that independently selects each prime $p$ with probability $1/p$. For a profile $f:2^P\to[0,1]$, put

$$
m(f)=\sum_{S\subseteq P}\mu(S)f(S),\qquad
Q(f\mid T)=\frac{\sum_{S\cap T=\varnothing}\mu(S)f(S)}
{\sum_{S\cap T=\varnothing}\mu(S)}.
$$

For $m(f)+m(g)>1$, define

$$
R(f,g)=\max\left\{
\max_{T:g(T)>0}Q(f\mid T),\
\max_{S:f(S)>0}Q(g\mid S)
\right\}.
$$

## Theorem

$$
\boxed{
\inf_{\substack{f,g:2^P\to[0,1]\\m(f)+m(g)>1}}
R(f,g)=\frac5{12}.
}
$$

No symmetry assumption $f=g$ is imposed. The fractional profiles may take arbitrary real values between zero and one.

## Proof

The monotone-compression theorem in the [fractional-sieve analysis](P040-fractional-sieve-variational.md) permits the restriction to coordinatewise increasing profiles without changing their masses or increasing $R$. Their positivity supports $U=\{S:f(S)>0\}$ and $V=\{T:g(T)>0\}$ are therefore upper sets of the five-dimensional Boolean lattice.

Let $D=\prod_{p\in P}p=15015$, and assign the integer weight

$$
w(S)=\prod_{p\in P\setminus S}(p-1),
\qquad \mu(S)=w(S)/D.
$$

For any support family $U$ and subset $T$ write

$$
M(U)=\sum_{S\in U}w(S),\qquad
C(U,T)=\sum_{\substack{S\in U\\S\cap T=\varnothing}}w(S),\qquad
H(T)=\sum_{S\cap T=\varnothing}w(S).
$$

Suppose $R(f,g)\le5/12$. For every $T\in V$, the fraction of selected mass of $f$ supported on masks disjoint from $T$ is at most $5/12$ of the entire disjoint mass. Because $f=0$ outside $U$ and $0\le f\le1$,

$$
12D\,m(f)\le
\min\left\{12M(U),\
\min_{T\in V}\left[12(M(U)-C(U,T))+5H(T)\right]\right\}
=:B(U,V).
$$

Similarly, $12D\,m(g)\le B(V,U)$. It suffices to establish the finite integer inequality

$$
\boxed{B(U,V)+B(V,U)\le12D}
\tag{1}
$$

for every pair of upper sets with $M(U)+M(V)>D$. Other support pairs already force $m(f)+m(g)\le1$.

The upper sets on $k$ coordinates are obtained inductively by choosing two upper sets $U_0,U_1$ on $k-1$ coordinates with $U_0\subseteq U_1$, representing the sections without and with the final coordinate. There are exactly $7581$ upper sets on five coordinates. Of their $7581^2$ ordered pairs, exactly $15586$ have $M(U)+M(V)>D$.

The [exact integer verifier](../experiments/P040_five_prime_sharpness.py) checks (1) for every one of these $15586$ pairs; all comparisons hold. No floating-point arithmetic or optimization solver is used. For an independent screening, the weaker general inequality

$$
12D\,m(f)\le
\min\left\{12M(U),\
\min_{T\in V}[12D-7H(T)]\right\}
$$

eliminates all but $12$ unordered support pairs, which (1) also eliminates. The verifier records both counts.

Thus $R(f,g)\le5/12$ implies $m(f)+m(g)\le1$, contrary to admissibility. Hence every admissible pair has $R(f,g)>5/12$.

For the reverse inequality on the infimum, take the near-extremal fractional profiles on $\{3,5,7\}$ established in the [fractional-sieve theorem](P040-fractional-sieve-variational.md) and extend them independently of the coordinates $11$ and $13$. Their selected masses and all relevant conditional disjointness probabilities remain unchanged. These examples approach $5/12$ from above. Consequently the infimum is exactly $5/12$. $\square$

## Verification and limitations

The certificate is reproduced with:

    python experiments/P040_five_prime_sharpness.py

The supporting unit test uses the same complete exact verification. The finite algorithm, its upper-set recurrence, and the explicit inequality (1) constitute a computer-assisted proof. The theorem does **not** establish $\lim_n\rho_n$, the full infinite-prime sieve infimum, or equality between the two limits. A sixth prime or an arithmetic Hall obstruction not generated by a fixed finite prime support may alter the threshold.

The [four-prime sharpness theorem](P040-four-prime-sharpness.md) is a direct predecessor. Related biased product-measure intersection inequalities include Wu and Feng, *Rigidity and stability for biased cross-intersecting families* (2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26871. This reference does not certify novelty of the present conditional Hall functional.
