# Symbolic mathematical prizes

The Scottish Book included playful rewards for some mathematical problems. This contemporary collection adopts the idea as an invitation to collaborate, not as a payment scheme. For historical context see [The Scottish Book, ed. R. Daniel Mauldin](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-319-22897-6).

Possible symbolic rewards include:

- A virtual cup of coffee ☕
- A virtual goose 🪿
- A theorem-shaped biscuit
- A contributor-proposed symbolic prize, subject to editorial review

**None of these is a material offer.** They acknowledge mathematics, not the quantity of submissions. A problem has no reward by default. A contributor may suggest a reward in the proposal form, but that suggestion does not create an obligation or imply acceptance.

A material prize may be displayed only when a named willing sponsor has separately confirmed the offer and its terms, including what counts as a satisfactory solution, eligibility, fulfillment, and any jurisdictional restrictions. The project itself makes no payment guarantee.

Recognition may be given for a complete proof, decisive counterexample, verified correction, or significant structural result. Historical prior-art discovery is also valued: discovering that a conjecture is already settled is a valid mathematical contribution. Public credit should distinguish proposer, solver, formalizer, verifier, and bibliographic contributor as supported by the record.
